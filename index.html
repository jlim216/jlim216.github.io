Welcome! I am a second-year PhD student and a Raymond Vernon Fellow in the Department of Government at Harvard University. I study the international political economy of security, focusing on how interactions between states and private actors shape global interdependence. Methodologically, my research focuses on causal inference and computational methods for high-dimensional unstructured data.

Before graduate school, I worked across academia and industry, using large-scale data to study questions in computational social science. My experience includes large-scale text analysis of congressional speeches at the Stanford Asia-Pacific Research Center, research on lobbying and political influence with MIT LobbyView, and internships with the AI research teams at Samsung Electronics and Salesforce. I also served as a squad leader in the Republic of Korea Army, where I led daily training sessions in English for cadets and received the Superintendent’s Award for Distinguished Service.

I graduated from Stanford University with an M.S. in Computer Science with a concentration in artificial intelligence and a B.A.S. with honors in Mathematical and Computational Science and International Relations. My honors thesis, completed through the Center for International Security and Cooperation, received awards from the Stanford Center for East Asian Studies and the Hoover Institution.

You can contact me by email at jblim@g.harvard.edu.
